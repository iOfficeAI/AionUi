## FACTOR DEEP DIVES

### Value Factor

**Current State**: Cheap vs history — the value spread (P/B ratio between value and growth quintiles) is at the 72nd percentile of historical wideness, meaning value stocks are historically cheap relative to growth stocks.

**Spread**: P/E spread between value (bottom quintile) and growth (top quintile) = 38x (value P/E ~12x, growth P/E ~50x). Historical average: ~22x. The spread has been wide for 4+ years.

**Macro Driver**: The AI capex cycle is extending the "growth premium" — investors are paying up for companies that grow in a slower-growth economy. Easing monetary policy (which benefits long-duration growth assets more) further disadvantages value. Rising rates would narrow this spread quickly.

**Crowding Risk**: Low — value is under-owned. Consensus positioning is anti-value. This is contrarian but requires a macro catalyst (rising rates, recession recovery rotation) to close.

**Outlook**: **Neutral** — the value spread is historically extreme, suggesting mean reversion risk, but the macro catalyst (rising rates or growth scare) is not in the immediate forecast. Patient value investors may find setup interesting for a 2027+ trade.

---

### Momentum Factor

**Current State**: **Strong** — momentum has been the top-performing factor for 3 consecutive years, driven primarily by AI infrastructure names (NVDA, AVGO, MRVL) and their spillover into related sectors.

**Recent Winner Sectors**: Semiconductors (AI), Technology Services, Industrials (data center construction, power)

**Volatility Regime**: Stable-Low (VIX 16.2) — momentum performs best in stable volatility regimes. When VIX rises above 25, momentum strategies typically experience crash risk as positions reverse.

**Crash Risk**: **Medium** — the current momentum basket (AI semis) is concentrated in a single theme. Momentum crashes occur when the dominant theme reverses — AI capex pause or China export shock would trigger simultaneous momentum reversal.

**Crowding Risk**: **High** — institutional long/short funds, quant momentum strategies, and retail investors are all positioned in the same AI names. Crowding creates crash risk when sentiment shifts.

**Outlook**: **Bullish near-term, Cautious medium-term** — momentum continues while AI capex data is positive (Q1 hyperscaler earnings in April will be key). Monitor VIX closely; any spike above 22 warrants reducing momentum positions.

---

### Quality Factor

**Current State**: **Favored** — in the current "slow Goldilocks" regime, investors prefer high-quality businesses (high ROE, low leverage, stable earnings) because they can grow without relying on capital markets.

**Key Metrics**: High ROE (>20%), low leverage (D/E <0.5), stable earnings growth (low revision risk). Current quality premium: quality stocks trade at 6.2x P/S vs the market's 3.1x P/S — a 100% premium that is near historical highs.

**Macro Context**: Quality outperforms in late cycle and early recession environments as investors pay up for earnings certainty. We are in late expansion/pre-recessionary territory (yield curve slightly inverted) — quality thesis remains intact.

**Overlap with Growth**: In the current cycle, quality and growth are unusually aligned (NVDA, MSFT, GOOGL all score high on both). Historically these factors compete; currently they reinforce.

**Outlook**: **Bullish** — quality premium likely to persist as late-cycle dynamics favor earnings visibility. NVDA (ROE 119%, D/E 0.43x) scores in the top decile of quality factors despite being a growth name.

---

### Low Volatility Factor

**Current State**: **Underperforming** — low-vol stocks (utilities, consumer staples, REITs) have lagged in a risk-on environment where investors are chasing AI growth. VIX at 16.2 signals complacency; low-vol factor typically needs VIX >25 to outperform.

**VIX Environment**: Low (16.2 vs 25+ needed for defensive outperformance). The low-vol premium is effectively negative in the current regime.

**Rate Sensitivity**: Low-vol stocks are rate-sensitive (they behave like bonds); Fed easing is a mild positive, but the magnitude of AI growth outperformance dwarfs the rate benefit.

**Outlook**: **Bearish near-term** — no catalyst for defensive outperformance until VIX regime shift. Investors seeking protection should consider direct hedges (put options, cash) rather than tilting into low-vol equities.

---

### Size Factor (Small vs Large Cap)

**Spread**: Small cap P/E discount to large cap: -32% (small caps trading at 32% discount). Historical average: -18% discount. Small caps are historically cheap vs large.

**Economic Sensitivity**: Small caps are highly cyclical (more domestic, more floating-rate debt). In the current late-expansion environment, small caps face higher refinancing costs (floating rate debt at 8-9%) and recession risk premium.

**Rate Impact**: Fed easing is a direct positive for small caps (much more floating rate debt than large caps). Each 25bp cut reduces small cap interest expense by an estimated $14B in aggregate.

**Outlook**: **Neutral-Bullish** — small caps are cheap and benefit from rate cuts, but execution risk and economic sensitivity create headwinds. IWM is interesting for a 6-12 month view as Fed easing cycle delivers 1-2 more cuts.

## ACADEMIC FOUNDATION

- **Value**: Fama-French (1992) — cheap stocks (low P/B, P/E) earn excess returns over time; premium partially explained by distress risk
- **Momentum**: Jegadeesh-Titman (1993) — 12-1 month winners continue to outperform; behavioral explanation: investor underreaction to news
- **Quality**: Novy-Marx (2013) — high gross profitability predicts strong returns; counter-intuitive because "expensive" quality stocks still outperform
- **Low-Vol**: Black, Jensen, Scholes (1972) — low-beta stocks outperform risk-adjusted; violates CAPM; explained by leverage constraints and agency issues
- **Size**: Banz (1981) — small caps earn premium; weakened significantly since being published (likely arbitraged away)
- **Growth** (modern): Not a classical factor, but MSCI, S&P, and Russell all publish growth indexes; empirically strong in low-rate, innovation-cycle environments
