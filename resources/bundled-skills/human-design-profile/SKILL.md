# Human Design Profile

Human Design maps a birth moment into a **bodygraph**: a Type, a decision-making Strategy and Authority,
a Profile (two lines), and a pattern of defined and undefined energy Centers. It is not fortune-telling.
Used honestly it is an **orientation lens** — a structured way to ask *"how is this person built to make
decisions, spend energy, and lead?"* — that gives grounding when someone is facing a private or business
choice. The output is not a personality horoscope — it is **a concrete decision-orientation for the actual
question on the table.**

## When to use
- The user wants grounding or orientation before a private or business decision and asks for it through this lens.
- "Give me my Human Design", "what's my type", "read my chart", "how am I designed to decide?"
- The user wants to align a choice (a hire, a launch, a partnership, a pivot, a relationship) to how they are built.
- The operator wants to understand how a client, partner, co-founder, or team member is wired before working with them.

## The method
1. **Collect the three inputs — exactly.** Birth **date**, birth **time** (as precise as possible — minutes
   matter; the Profile and several Centers shift with the hour), and birth **place** (city/country, so the
   **timezone and historical DST** can be resolved). If the time is unknown, say so plainly and offer a
   time-uncertain reading (Type and most Centers are usually stable; Profile and Authority may be ambiguous) —
   do not silently guess a time.
2. **Compute the chart — never fabricate it.** Human Design uses **two** calculations: the **Personality/conscious**
   placement at the birth moment, and the **Design/unconscious** placement at **~88° of solar arc before birth**
   (roughly 88 days earlier). Run both through a real Human Design calculation engine — an HD API or library, or
   an ephemeris-based HD calculator. If no calculation engine is available, **do not invent gates, channels, or a
   type.** Say so, ask for the data, and compute when an engine is reachable. A guessed bodygraph is worse than no
   reading.
3. **Present the chart plainly.** State, in plain language: **Type** (Generator / Manifesting-Generator / Projector /
   Manifestor / Reflector) · **Strategy** (respond · respond-then-inform · wait-for-the-invitation · inform-then-act ·
   wait-a-lunar-cycle) · **Inner Authority** (Sacral / Emotional / Splenic / Ego / Self-projected / Mental / Lunar) ·
   **Profile** (the two lines, e.g. 1/3, 4/6, 5/1) · **defined vs. undefined Centers** and the **Definition**
   (single / split / etc.) · the **key Gates and Channels** that shape this person. No jargon-dumping — name it, then
   say what it means in one human sentence each.
4. **Apply it to the actual question.** This is the point of the skill. Translate the chart into concrete
   decision-orientation for what the user is actually deciding. Examples of the move: *"As a Generator/MG you're
   built to **respond**, not to initiate from a cold start — so test this offer against a real signal (an inbound, a
   gut-yes) before you build it."* · *"Emotional Authority means there's **no truth in the now** — sleep on this
   pricing decision across an emotional wave before you commit."* · *"Undefined Will/Ego — stop making promises about
   what you'll deliver under pressure; structure the deal so you don't have to prove your worth."* · *"Projector —
   your leadership lands **when invited and recognized**; chasing un-invited B2B outreach will burn you out, so build
   for referral and recognition instead."* Connect each piece of the design to a lean-into and an avoid.
5. **Frame it honestly.** Close by naming what this is: an **orientation and reflection lens**, not a deterministic
   prediction. It gives grounding and a vocabulary for noticing how you operate — it does not tell the user what
   *will* happen or remove their agency. Tone throughout: **grounding, plain, useful — not woo, not mystical
   authority, not flattery.** If the chart suggests something the user won't want to hear, say it kindly and plainly.

## Output
- The **bodygraph summary** — Type · Strategy · Inner Authority · Profile · defined/undefined Centers · Definition · key Gates/Channels, each with a one-line plain meaning.
- The **decision-orientation** applied to the user's actual question: how they're designed to decide here, what to lean into, what to avoid, and the one practical move that follows.
- A short, honest **framing line**: this is an orientation/reflection lens for grounding, not a prediction.

## For Command EVE
EVE offers this natively as a confidant/orientation capability for the operator ("Alois") — and as something Alois can
in turn offer the people he creates in the product.

1. **Alois self-checks first.** On request, EVE collects Alois's birth date/time/place, computes his chart through the
   HD calculation engine (never fabricated), and gives him his Type/Strategy/Authority/Profile **applied to a real
   decision he's facing in the business** — e.g. how he's designed to make the call he's stuck on today.
2. **Then Alois checks the people he creates.** Once Alois has created a client, partner, or team member in the product,
   EVE can run the same lens on **them** (with their birth data and consent), so Alois understands how that person is
   wired before he sells to, partners with, or manages them — and so the reading is framed *for that working relationship.*
3. **EVE creates the profile/asset.** EVE produces the chart and the written orientation as a durable artifact (a saved
   profile / one-pager) attached to that person's record, so it persists and can be revisited when the next decision comes up.
4. **Boundaries.** Birth data is personal data — handle it under the same privacy posture as any PII (EU/local where
   required, consent before reading a third party). Always deliver it as grounding and reflection, never as a deterministic
   verdict about a person's worth, hireability, or fate.
