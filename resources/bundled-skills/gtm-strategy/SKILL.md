# GTM Strategy

Go-to-market is the bridge between "we built a thing" and "a specific person with budget pays us, again and again".
It is **not** a marketing plan — it is a set of **coupled decisions** (one ICP, one frame, one price floor, one channel,
one beachhead) that only work together. The job here is to make those decisions *grounded in evidence* and *stress-tested
before spend*, then lock them with explicit kill-criteria so you know when you're wrong fast. This skill is the conductor:
it pulls **deep-research** for evidence, **icp-persona-panel** to validate the offer, and **pre-mortem** to attack the plan,
then hands the locked GTM to **plan-system** as a versioned roadmap.

## When to use
- Bringing a product (or a new product line / market) to market for the first time.
- Choosing a target group or a market to enter — or re-deciding when the current motion isn't converting.
- Designing the acquisition motion: where to find buyers, how to reach them cheaply, how to manufacture proof.
- When the user asks "who do we sell to / how do we sell this / how do we reach them / which market first / what do we charge".

## The method
Run it in order — each step feeds the next. Do not skip the evidence step and jump to a price; that's how toy GTMs die.

1. **RESEARCH the market, competitors, and ICP** — via **deep-research** (all three modes). Get current, sourced evidence:
   market size + timing, who each competitor targets and how they price, and — the load-bearing output — **the gap competitors
   leave unserved** and the "tried-AI-and-got-stuck" buyer signal per market. Refute soft numbers before building on them.
2. **POSITIONING — one ICP, one use-case, one frame, named MOAT.** Collapse to a single sentence: *for [one ICP] who [one
   pain], we are the [category] that [one outcome], unlike [incumbent].* Then name the **MOAT** — the *structural* thing rivals
   can't copy (bundle, orchestration, data-isolation, distribution, switching-cost), **not** price. If your only edge is "cheaper",
   you don't have a position, you have a countdown.
3. **RANK ICPs per market → pick the BEACHHEAD.** Score each candidate ICP (per market) on **pain × solvency × reachability ×
   entry-speed × product-fit × competition-gap**. Then overlay **FOUNDER-FIT** — assets, network, language, existing warm doors —
   because the right beachhead is the one *you* can win fastest and cheapest, not the biggest TAM. Pick the segment that yields the
   first paying customers soonest.
4. **PRICING / PACKAGING — floor tier + per-unit expansion.** Set a **floor tier** plus **per-unit expansion** (packs/usage), and
   **kill the unlimited tier** — it is the structural margin-drain that one heavy user turns into a per-customer loss. Price *signals*
   tier: at delivery-grade, too-cheap reads as "toy" and repels the solvent buyer. Land-and-expand beats a big upfront number.
5. **CHANNEL MOTION — go where they already are + manufacture the first reference.** Prefer **teach-led / creator-rented /
   warm-intro** over expensive paid (cheaper trust, faster proof). Then build the **reference-customer proof loop**: a real
   **60–90-day no-failure reference + peer-proof** beats any deck — so *manufacture the first reference deliberately* (hand-pick one
   ideal-fit customer, over-serve, document the outcome) and let it pull the next ones.
6. **VALIDATE then STRESS-TEST before commit.** Run **icp-persona-panel** on the actual offer/price/headline (does the solvent
   buyer say yes, on whom, where) and **pre-mortem** on the whole plan (assume it failed in 12 months — why?). Fix what they catch
   *before* you spend, not after. Only then lock.

## Output
- **Locked positioning** (the one-sentence frame) + the **named MOAT**.
- **ICP ranking per market** (the scored grid) + the chosen **beachhead**, with the founder-fit reason it's reachable first.
- **Pricing/packaging** (floor tier + expansion units, no unlimited) + the price-as-signal rationale.
- **Channel plan** (the 1–2 real motions) + the **first-customers sequence** and the manufactured-reference play.
- **Kill-criteria** — the metrics/dates that say "this ICP/price/channel is wrong, pivot" — so being wrong is cheap and visible.

## Key discipline
- **Evidence, not assertion.** Every load-bearing claim (market size, what a competitor charges, where the ICP hangs out) is a
  deep-research finding with a source — or it's flagged as a hypothesis to test, never stated as fact.
- **One beachhead, not a spread.** "We'll sell to everyone" is the failure mode. Name *one* segment in *one* market first; the
  rest is the v2 roadmap, not the launch.
- **Founder-fit overrides generic TAM.** The biggest market you can't reach is worth less than the small one you can win this month.
- **Pretty distribution ≠ solvent buyer.** Reach/clicks don't pay rent. Carry the solvent-buyer verdict from the persona panel
  through to the final pricing call.

## For Command EVE
This is a native EVE capability — the strategy brain a fractional CMO/founder-advisor would run, orchestrated through Hermes as a
chain of the other skills. Governed by **eve-doctrine**: EVE acts as confidant/challenger, gates anything irreversible, and keeps
every client's GTM **isolated** (never reuse one client's research or references for another).

- **EVE builds the GTM as a steerable artifact.** EVE runs the deep-research, drafts the positioning + ICP ranking + pricing +
  channel plan, then writes it into **plan-system** as a versioned roadmap (v1 beachhead → v2 expansion) the operator keeps steering.
- **Alois scopes himself first.** Before he commits, the operator (Alois) runs his *own* GTM through EVE — his beachhead, his price,
  his first-reference play — validated by the persona panel and attacked by the pre-mortem (this mirrors how the founder war-gamed
  the single-79€ + credits model and killed the unlimited tier before committing).
- **Then Alois scopes each client.** For every client he takes on, Alois points the same chain at *their* market and offer — EVE
  produces a per-client locked GTM with kill-criteria, isolated from every other client's data.
- **Evidence rule.** Any number that isn't a sourced deep-research finding or a persona-panel reaction is a hypothesis to validate
  with a real test (a live ad, a price experiment, a first reference) — GTM narrows the bet, the market settles it.
