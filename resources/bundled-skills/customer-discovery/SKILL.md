# Customer Discovery

Most "market research" is a founder guessing what customers value, dressing the guess as a feature list, and never
checking. Customer discovery inverts that: you ask a small number of **real** customers two ruthlessly concrete
questions, **listen instead of explaining**, write their words down verbatim, and condense the pattern into **ONE
value-prop sentence** that becomes the filter for every decision and the headline of every page. It is the cheapest,
highest-signal step before you spend a cent on the offer or the funnel — and the one founders avoid most.

This is **real-customer** discovery. It is the truth-source that the simulated `icp-persona-panel` *approximates* when
you have no customers yet — and the panel becomes far sharper once seeded with what real buyers actually said here.

## When to use
- **Validating an offer, positioning, or price** — when the value prop is still an assumption, not a quote.
- When the user asks "what do my customers actually want", "would they pay for this", "why do they really stay/leave".
- **Before** writing the landing page (`landing-copy`) — the page should sell the *validated* frame, not the guessed one.
- After churn, a failed launch, or a pivot — to find the *one thing* that was actually load-bearing.
- On a recurring cadence (monthly pulse, quarterly pattern-pass, every offboarding) to keep the value prop honest.

## The method
Run it as phases — generate the script, run the interviews, pattern-analyse, distill the sentence. Listening is the work.

1. **Dismantle the fear first.** Founders skip this step because they fear negative feedback, assume they already know,
   and believe more delivery is better — usually all three are wrong. Reframe: most customers are *flattered* to be
   asked; 20 minutes is mutual benefit, not waste; the ones who'd churn will anyway, and asking early often prevents it;
   the answers that sting are exactly the ones you'd have paid a consultant for. Permission to be uncomfortable is granted.
2. **Generate the interview script.** Keep it tiny. Opener: *"I'm making sure we focus our energy on the right things —
   could you give me 20 minutes for two questions? Not what you think I want to hear — the honest answer."* Then the
   **Two Magic Questions**, verbatim, in order:
   - **Take-away (Q1):** *"What is the ONE thing — if I took it away — would make this not valuable to you anymore?"*
     → reveals the **real core value prop** (not what *you* think makes it valuable).
   - **Add-on (Q2):** *"What is the ONE thing — if I added it — would make you stay forever?"*
     → reveals the **next lever**, where pain or a gap still lives.
   Force *one* thing each — five wishes is wishlist noise; one forces the customer to prioritize what truly matters.
3. **Run / guide the interviews — and LISTEN, don't explain.** Aim for ~10 customers, *including former ones*. The single
   most common failure is explaining/justifying/solving the moment they answer — every explanation contaminates the
   answer. The operator's only job in the call: tolerate silence, ask follow-ups, write **verbatim** (exact words, never
   paraphrased). Follow-ups when an answer is vague: *"Tell me more about that." · "What does that look like day-to-day?" ·
   "When did you last notice it?" · "How much is that worth to you?"* After each call: 5 min — what surprised you?
4. **Pattern-analyse the verbatims.** After ~10 calls, lay the answers side by side. Which Q1 answer recurred across
   customers? That convergence *is* the core value (at Telos all 30 agents independently said "saves me a day a week").
   Cluster Q2 answers into the next-lever shortlist. Name what *no one* mentioned — that's the complexity you build for
   yourself, not for them. Mark each pattern: many-customer **FACT**, single-quote **SIGNAL**, or founder **HYPOTHESIS**.
5. **Distill the ONE value-prop sentence.** Compose the top Q1 pattern into a single sentence with three parts: a
   **concrete measurable unit** ("one day a week"), an **emotionally scaling form** ("52 days a year"), and a **reframe
   question** ("what would you do with that?"). Then run the two tests: the **filter test** — can you decide every live
   initiative, hire, and ad-spend by asking "does this serve [the prop]?" (if not, it's too vague — sharpen) — and the
   **mirror test** — read it back to 3 customers: *"Would you describe us this way?"* A feature list ("we do editing,
   social, web, VFX…") filters nothing and is forgettable; the single sentence is the operational spine.

## Output
- The **interview script** (opener + the two questions + the follow-up bank).
- The **verbatim log** — each customer's Q1/Q2 answers in their exact words, plus the per-call "what surprised me".
- The **pattern analysis** — recurring Q1 (core value) and clustered Q2 (next levers), each marked FACT / SIGNAL / HYPOTHESIS, plus what nobody asked for.
- The **one validated value-prop sentence** (concrete unit + scaling form + reframe), passed through the filter test and the mirror test.
- The decision it changes: the value-frame the page (`landing-copy`) sells, the next thing to build, and the seed for the persona panel.

## Key discipline
- **Listen, don't explain.** The instant you justify, you've taught the customer your answer and lost theirs. Silence and follow-ups only.
- **Verbatim, not paraphrase.** Record exact words — paraphrase smuggles in the founder's hypothesis.
- **Force "one thing".** A list is noise; the forced single answer is the priority. Hold the line on Q1 and Q2.
- **Real customers, including former ones.** Exit interviews carry the sharpest signal about what was actually missing.
- **A value prop that filters nothing is too vague.** If you can't decide spend/hires/cuts with it, it isn't done.

## For Command EVE
This is a native EVE capability — EVE acts as the customer-research hire the operator (Alois) doesn't have time to be.

- **EVE creates the asset.** EVE generates the tailored interview script, holds the verbatim log as the operator runs the
  calls, pattern-condenses the answers, and drafts the one-sentence value prop with the filter + mirror tests applied —
  saved so it can be re-run on the recurring cadence as the offer evolves (this can live as a milestone in `plan-system`).
- **Alois discovers his own value first.** Before writing his page or his ads, the operator runs discovery on his *own*
  customers; EVE turns the verbatims into the value-frame, then hands it straight to `landing-copy` as the validated hero
  promise — and into `icp-persona-panel` to seed the personas with what real buyers said (real signal beats simulated).
- **Then Alois runs it on his clients.** For each client he serves in EVE, EVE generates the client-specific script and
  condenses *their* customers' answers into *their* value prop — invisible-delivery, the client's brand, per the doctrine.
- **Honesty rule (see `eve-doctrine`).** EVE never lets a founder hypothesis pass as a customer fact: anything not grounded
  in a verbatim is flagged HYPOTHESIS and routed to a real call or an `icp-persona-panel` run, not asserted. When the
  operator must commit, `decision-brief` compresses the patterns into one call. Deeper market context comes via `deep-research`.
